# Ruleset: unslop

For documents the reader receives.

Derived from the unslop skill at https://github.com/cursor/plugins/blob/main/pstack/skills/unslop/SKILL.md

## Mechanical

- Remove em dashes. End the sentence or use a comma. Do not substitute parentheses or en dashes.
- Replace curly quotes with straight quotes.
- Convert headings to sentence case.
- Remove decorative emoji.
- Remove chatbot phrases and sycophancy: "I hope this helps", "Great question", "Certainly".
- Restore the copula: "serves as" and "stands as" become "is", "boasts" becomes "has".
- Convert inline-header lists that restate their own label into prose. A bold lead-in followed by genuinely new detail is fine.
- Cut filler: "in order to", "due to the fact that", "it is important to note that".
- Collapse stacked hedging: "could potentially possibly" becomes "may".
- Replace fancy synonyms: utilize, leverage, facilitate, numerous, in the event that.
- Cut adverbs propping up weak verbs. Use a stronger verb or the number.
- Convert passive to active where the actor is known.

## Judgement

- Sentences with no position where the author clearly holds one. Propose the position. Never invent an opinion.
- First person. If the document avoids "I" through impersonal or passive constructions and the author is plainly the actor, report it and ask whether the document should speak in first person. Ask once, for the document, not once per sentence. Apply it only if the author says yes, or told you at the start. Never switch voice on your own, and never claim as one person's view something the document attributes to a team.
- Uniform sentence rhythm. Propose splits and merges.
- Abstract framing that could be a mechanism or a number instead.
- Boldface applied to every proper noun or acronym.
- Significance inflation: "pivotal", "testament to", "evolving landscape".
- Promotional language: "robust", "seamless", "powerful", "elegant".
- Vague attribution: "experts suggest", "it is widely held".
- Formulaic contrast: "despite the challenges, X continues to".
- Negative parallelism: "not just X, but Y".
- Ideas forced into groups of three.
- False ranges, where the two endpoints share no scale.
- Generic conclusions: "the future looks bright".
- Abstract metaphor nouns: substrate, vector, nexus, bedrock, surface, primitive as a noun.
